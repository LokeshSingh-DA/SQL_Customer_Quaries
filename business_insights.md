## Key Findings

### 1. Clothing generates the highest sales value

Clothing generated the highest value of ordered merchandise, while Electronics led in units sold.

| Category | Units sold | Sales value |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Clothing | 559 | ₹1,937,607.42 |
| Electronics | 687 | ₹1,863,063.51 |
| Books | 350 | ₹1,118,991.06 |
| Home | 443 | ₹1,064,051.76 |
| Toys | 405 | ₹977,259.91 |

**Business implication:** Clothing is the largest revenue contributor, while Electronics has the strongest purchase volume. Merchandise, inventory, and campaign decisions should consider both measures rather than relying on revenue alone.

### 2. Order statuses require attention

| Status | Orders | Share of orders | Order value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Delivered | 105 | 26.2% | ₹2,022,410.31 |
| Shipped | 102 | 25.5% | ₹1,607,085.03 |
| Cancelled | 99 | 24.8% | ₹1,651,736.62 |
| Pending | 94 | 23.5% | ₹1,679,741.70 |

Nearly one in four orders is marked **Cancelled**. This is a meaningful operational signal: the business should investigate cancellation reasons such as stock availability, delivery timelines, payment failures, or customer drop-off.

### 7. Reviews present an opportunity to improve feedback coverage

All 50 products have been ordered, but only 34 products have at least one review. This leaves **16 products without customer feedback**.

**Business implication:** Send a post-purchase review request for under-reviewed products. More review coverage can strengthen product discovery and support product-quality decisions.
